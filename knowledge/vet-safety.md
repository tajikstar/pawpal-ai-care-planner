## Emergencies
Contact a veterinarian or emergency animal hospital right away if a pet has trouble breathing, collapses, has a seizure, is bleeding heavily, cannot urinate, or may have eaten something toxic. For suspected poisoning, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center can be reached at (888) 426-4435 (a fee may apply).

## Common toxic foods
Chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, xylitol (a sweetener in some gum and peanut butter) and alcohol are dangerous to pets. Keep them out of reach.

## Medication safety
Only give a pet medication that a veterinarian has prescribed or approved, at the dose the vet specified. Many human medicines are toxic to pets, and doses that are safe for people can be dangerous for animals. A care planner can remind you to give medication at the time your vet set, but the drug and dose must come from your vet.

## Routine
