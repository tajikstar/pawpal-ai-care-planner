## Health checks
Senior cats should see a veterinarian more often than younger cats; many vets recommend a checkup every six months. Kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, dental disease and arthritis are more common in older cats.

## Kidney health and water
Cats with kidney disease often drink and urinate more. Keeping fresh water easy to reach in more than one spot encourages drinking. Any special kidney diet or medication must come from your veterinarian; do not change diet or give medication without their guidance.

## Feeding
Some senior cats eat better with smaller, more frequent meals. Track appetite and weight, and tell your vet about weight loss or changes in eating.

## Comfort and mobility
Older cats may struggle with high places or tall litter boxes. A low-sided litter box, soft bedding and steps up to favorite spots help.
